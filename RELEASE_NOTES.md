Release candidate 13. A test release: the CVN Testnet has not started, and coins on any CVN test network have no value and carry over to nothing.

**This release changes the rules, in four places, and it does not agree with release candidate 12 or with any release before it.** A network that runs it must be started afresh. A chain file written by an older release is not carried over, and this program refuses it when it starts. Every machine on a network must run this release, because an older program refuses some of the blocks this one accepts. The development network (`devnet`) starts again from its first block, and the coins on it from before are gone. They had no value. Wallets are not touched: an address and its private key work as before.

The rest of what is new: a node can now be a pool, and a miner can mine at one; a backup is worked out again from its blocks before it is put back; a node refuses to start on a chain file it cannot trust; and a security audit of the whole program found five faults, all now fixed. All of it has been read by AI reviewers and by nobody else. "What is known and not done", near the end, is the part to read before relying on any of it.

## The four rules that changed

Each of these holds from the first block of a network. None has a starting height. That is why no older chain can be carried over. They are written out in `WHITEPAPER.md`, and `audit/CHANGES_FOR_THE_RESTART.md` states the last three as claims for a reviewer to test.

- **A block's reward may pay up to fifty addresses, each once.** A reward used to have one output. It may now have as many as fifty, each to a different address, each at least 0.0001 CVN, and together no more than the block may pay. What a block does not pay stays in the vault, as before. A miner working alone is not affected: its reward has one output. The reason is pools. A pool can now pay the miners who did the work inside the block itself, and so never holds their coins. This was the one thing about pools that could not be added once a network had started.
- **Every fee in a block goes to its miner, and none to the vault.** Until now, while new coins were still being issued, a fifth of every fee went to the vault. Now the miner may claim the new reward, the vault's payout and all of the fees, at every height. A miner may still claim less than a block allows, and what it leaves goes to the vault. That is the miner's choice.
- **A fifth of every block is closed to transfers that pay a fee.** A transfer pays when its fee is more than nothing. In one block at most 400 transfers may pay, and those that pay may together measure at most 240,000 of the block's 300,000 bytes. A block that breaks either limit is not valid. The reason: someone moving or renewing their own coins should not have to buy a place in a block, and no rule can force a miner to include a transfer. What a rule can do is take away the gain from leaving free transfers out. The last fifth of a block cannot be sold. What it cannot do: a sender and a miner who know each other can settle outside the fee, and such a transfer counts as free. And when many paying transfers are waiting and no free ones, the last fifth of the block stays empty. The rule has no end date.
- **A fifth swept from a coin stays on the coin, set aside for its owner, for seven more years.** A coin left unspent for seven years used to lose a fifth of its amount to the vault at once, and an owner who missed the date by a day lost it for good. Now the fifth is taken off the coin's amount and set aside on the coin itself. Spending the coin in that time, to anyone or to its own address, gives its owner the amount and the fifth together. If nobody comes for it, the fifth goes to the vault at the coin's next date, and a new fifth is then set aside. A coin too small for a new fifth has none taken. A transfer signed before that second date and still waiting is no longer valid after it, because the coin has changed. Every coin, plus everything set aside on coins, plus the vault, still adds up to exactly what has been issued, after every block.

## For someone joining a network

- **Use this release, and no other, on a network that runs it.** Older programs and this one cannot share a chain.
- **Start from an empty chain.** If you have a chain file from an older release (`cvn_devnet.db` on the development network), delete it. Your wallet is not in that file. The copies in the `backups` folder come from the old chain too: do not put one back. If you forget, this program will not start on the old file. It says so and says what to do.
- Use the same Bitcoin height and the same seeds as the people who run the network give you, as before.

## For miners

- **A miner can mine at a pool.** Start with `--pool-mine HOST`, or put `"mine_at_pool"` in `miner_config.json`. This uses the fast miner (the CVN edition of XMRig), with your saved mining address as your name at the pool. No node runs on your machine and no chain is kept. The program first reads the pool's page and prints what the pool says of itself, and refuses a pool that is on another network. Once a minute it prints your machine's speed, your shares, and what the pool says your address has been paid, is owed and has on the way. The built-in miner cannot work for a pool, and the program never falls back to it. Section 5 of `MINING_GUIDE.md` has the details.
- **At a pool you receive a new coin with every block that pays you.** That can be hundreds a day. One transfer spends at most 200 coins, so use "Combine my coins" in the wallet before sending a large amount.
- **The status line counts blocks that are not yet sure.** A pool counts a block as sure once 100 more stand on it, about 75 minutes. Before, for that long a new miner's line said the blocks had paid it nothing while the wallet already held the coins.
- **Working alone is as it was.** A block's miner now keeps every fee in it. The program fills its own blocks to the new limits, so it cannot build a block that the rule refuses. The room it leaves in a block for the header and reward grew from 2,000 to 6,000 bytes so that a reward of fifty outputs fits. That is a habit of this program and not a rule.

## For someone running a pool

A pool is this program started with one more option, `--pool CVN_ADDRESS`. There is an installer, `deploy/install_pool.sh`, and a guide, `POOL_GUIDE.md`. `POOL_DESIGN.md` is the design, for outside readers.

- **Shares and a record.** The pool takes miners in on the work port. Each logs in with its own address and is given work sized for its machine, about one answer every 30 seconds. An answer that meets that size is a share. An answer that also meets the network's difficulty is a block. The pool keeps a record of every share, every block and what each address has been paid and is owed. A page at `/pool` (and `/pool.json`) shows it.
- **The block pays the miners itself.** The reward is divided among the most recent shares, counted back until their work is twice the work of one block, and each part of 0.01 CVN or more is paid inside the block, straight to its address. That uses the first rule above. Those coins go from the network to the miners and never pass through the pool, so nobody who runs a pool, or breaks into one, can keep them. A block has room for fifty addresses. If more than fifty are owed a part, the 49 with the most work are paid and the fiftieth output goes to the pool's own address.
- **Small parts are paid later.** Parts under 0.01 CVN, and the parts of miners beyond the fifty, go to the pool's address in the same block and are owed. They are the only coins a pool holds for anybody. A pool with few miners may hold none.
- **Paying is done by a separate command on a timer.** `cvn_node --pool-pay` runs every five hours (the installer sets the timer). It pays every address owed 1 CVN or more, and it also pays an address owed less, from 0.002 CVN up, once that address has gone twelve hours without a payment. So a small miner is reached within a day of the last payment. A transfer pays up to 49 addresses and costs 0.001 CVN, shared equally among them. The node that listens to miners holds no key. The key is in memory only for the seconds the command runs, in a program that listens to nobody. A payment that has been sent is never made twice.
- **The record is copied and checked.** The pool copies its record every hour, checks each copy, and keeps the newest 48. `--check-pool-backup` checks any copy and `--restore-pool-backup` puts one back. A record put back reads the chain again for the pool's own payments, so that a copy older than a payment does not pay it twice.
- **What this does not remove.** A miner's machine searches the work it is given without reading it. So a pool could hand out work that pays other addresses. It would show on the chain and on the pool page with every block, and cannot be hidden. An operator can also fail to pay what is owed, or keep it. That is limited to the small parts above, and the guides say so.
- **Three outside reviews of the pool** found faults in how the record, the payments and the installer worked. Each is fixed with a test. None let a stranger take a coin.

## For anyone running a seed

- **The seed service no longer runs as root.** `deploy/install_seed.sh` runs it as its own user, `cvn-seed`, and keeps its chain in `/var/lib/cvn_seed/NETWORK` (the network's name), with limits on what it may write and a memory limit for the program.
- **Moving an older seed across.** Run `update_seed.sh` first, naming the old chain file so that it is deleted: for the development network, `bash /opt/cvn_node/deploy/update_seed.sh cvn_devnet.db`. Then run `install_seed.sh` again, as you set the seed up. That second step makes the new user and moves what is left of the old seed's files across. Do the order this way because this release refuses the old chain.
- **`update_seed.sh` builds the new program before it stops the seeds**, so a build that fails leaves them running. It accepts a plain file name only. It used to be possible to make it delete a file outside its folder.
- **`install_seed.sh` is careful with files.** It never deletes a copy left by a run that was cut off, because after a reboot in between that copy can be the whole chain. It sets it aside and says so. It refuses a link at any name it touches in the seed's folder, and it stops with a clear message on a system whose Go is too old (Ubuntu 22.04).
- **These two scripts have not yet been run on a real server in their new form.** Only their syntax and the file-name check were tried. Do one seed first, look at it, and only then the others.

## For anyone running a node

- **A node refuses to start on a chain file it cannot trust.** It prints "DATABASE DAMAGED", the reason, and what to do. At start it checks that the stored height is there, is a height, is the last block the file holds, and that the newest block can be read. It checks that the first block is this network's first block in every part. It adds up every coin, everything set aside on coins and the vault, and the total must be exactly what has been issued through the newest block. And it checks that it can rebuild its totals of work. An outside reviewer tried to make a healthy node fail these checks and could not.
- **What it does not find at start:** a coin given to another address, with the totals left alone. Only the full check of a backup (below) finds that. A test pins this limit so it is not forgotten.
- **A damaged file now stops things instead of being passed over.** A record that has gone missing is damage, not an empty record. A block that cannot be read stops a rollback, which now removes all of its blocks or none. The total work of a chain is never added up across a block that is missing. These came from outside reviews of the new rules. Each round found something the round before had missed, until the last, which found nothing.
- **Putting a backup back now works the chain out again from its blocks first.** `--restore-backup` gives the backup's blocks one by one to the same checks a block from another node gets, into a scratch chain, and holds the backup to the result: every coin, the vault after every block, the total work through every block, and the records of what each block spent and swept. Only then is the file put in place, and it is held to the SHA-256 of the file that was checked. `--check-backup-fully FILE` does the same check by hand and can run while a node runs. The cost is time and disk space: every proof of work is repeated, so it takes longer the longer the chain is, and it needs free disk space about the size of the chain. **It has not been timed on a long chain.** The copy a node makes each day still gets only the quick check, which holds a file's records to each other and does not work the coins out again. Section 10 of `Installation_Guide.md` has the commands.
- **Security audit of the whole program.** It found five faults. All were real, none was in a rule, and all are fixed.
  1. Messages from strangers could use up a seed's memory. Large messages held at one time now share a fixed room, 16 MiB for strangers and 16 MiB for seeds, taken as the bytes arrive. A message that finds no room is not kept, and a connection with eight of those is closed.
  2. A wallet read a node's answer without limit. Every answer is now read to a limit in bytes and in list items.
  3. The mining work port had no limits when no pool was running. The pool's limits on connections and on answers without the work now hold for every miner that is not on the node's own machine.
  4. The seed service ran as root. See above.
  5. The update script could be made to delete a file outside its folder. See above.
- **Before that, a stranger could run a node out of memory.** One block message of 2 MB, made of empty transfers, decoded into 699,037 transactions and 623 MB of memory. Seeds run with a memory limit of 800 MB, so two such messages from one address could kill a seed, again after every restart. It needed no key (the network's message key is public), no mining and no money. This was in release candidate 12. A node now counts the items in a block or transfer before it decodes it, and refuses one with more items than a valid block can hold. Replies to a request for blocks are read to 24 MB and not 64. A list of other nodes is refused if it is longer than any node sends, and at most 16 newly named nodes are greeted from one list. No rule changed: every limit is at or above what the rules allow.
- **A limit that is left.** Whoever keeps the shared room for large messages from strangers full stops large blocks arriving from strangers. It does not stop blocks from seeds, and it does not stop small blocks. This is written down and was not changed.
- **Packages and build.** Four packages the program uses had 24 published security notices between them. Each is moved to its newest release (named echo, x/net, x/crypto and x/text). Three of them reach the program only through the wallet's window. The fourth, x/crypto, is used by the mining hash. No rule changed, and the RandomX tests that compare with known hashes pass unchanged. Every action in the build recipes is now named by its exact commit and not by a tag that can be moved, and GitHub proposes updates once a month for a person to accept. The program is still built with Go 1.27.4.
- **Where to report a fault privately.** On the repository's Security tab, "Report a vulnerability". The site says so at `/.well-known/security.txt`, and the issue template does too. No email address is involved.

## In the wallet

- **A private key that begins with zeros was taken for a password.** About one key in 256 begins with a zero byte, and release candidate 12 and earlier wrote such a key as 62 characters and not 64. On a computer with a key saved in `wallet_key.json`, anything shorter than 63 characters was taken for the saved key's password. So the owner of such a key who typed the key itself was told "that is not the password of the key saved on this computer". The 24 recovery words always worked, and so did the key on a computer with no saved key, so no coins were out of reach. A new key is now always written with all 64 characters. With a key saved, what you type is tried as the password first. If it does not open the saved key and is 56 to 64 characters of 0-9 and a-f, it is taken as a private key. Anything shorter is refused as a wrong password, as before. A second fault of the same kind is mended: a password of 63 or 64 characters of 0-9 and a-f, such as `openssl rand -hex 32` makes, never opened the saved key.
- **A sender may give something to a second address in the same transfer.** In either wallet you may name a second address and what to give it, an amount in CVN or a share of the amount being sent such as `10%`. It is one more output of an ordinary transfer, added to the amount, so the person you pay gets all of it. **This is not a rule of the chain.** A node cannot tell it from any other transfer to two addresses. Both wallets show the gift as a figure, and what leaves the wallet in all, before anything is sent. Nothing is suggested and nothing is remembered. With the two fields empty, a transfer is exactly what it was. In the text wallet the question comes after the key, which is typed unseen, and no refusal shows back what was typed where the address goes.
- **An amount typed with eleven digits of whole coins was not refused as too large.** It came out below nothing. Nothing could be sent that way, because everything that uses the figure refuses an amount below nothing, but it is now refused as more than can exist.
- **Renew and Combine send with no fee unless you ask to pay one,** because of the closed fifth of a block. The wallet notes each free renewal. If those coins are still unspent three days later it says so and offers to send the renewal again with a fee of 0.001 CVN for each transfer, or another you name. It shows the number of transfers and the total, and sends only on a plain yes. When a coin's date is less than two days away, it says that a free renewal may wait.
- **The wallets show what is set aside** on a coin, and until which block, and say what a renewal takes back.

## Reviewed and tested since the last release

- **The reviews were by AI reviewers.** Outside AI reviewers, Codex and Gemini, read the pool, the reward rule, and the three other rules changed for this restart, in several rounds, and one of them audited the security of the whole program. Each finding was fixed, with a test that fails without the fix. For the restart rules, the second reviewer then confirmed its own earlier findings closed, and its last pass found nothing. **In the reviews of the rules, no reviewer found a way to make or lose a coin with valid blocks.** Several findings were about a node going on with wrong coins after its own file had been damaged. None of those can be reached from the network. `audit/CHANGES_FOR_THE_RESTART.md` has the record for the restart rules.
- **There has been no review by an independent person or firm.** Nobody outside has read these changes except AI. The authors also used AI reviewers of the same kind as the one that wrote the code, and that is not an outside review. The rules need a human review before any public network.
- **Tests, on the code of this release.** The whole suite: 489 passed, none failed, 10 skipped. The same suite under Go's race detector: 489 passed, no race found. Tests that start real nodes on one machine, run twice over for this release: all 16 passed both times (32 of 32). A seventeenth, which needs the real fast miner, is skipped on the build machine and was not run. Tests for each rule were written before the code and shown to fail without it. Faults were planted one at a time in the new rules and the tests caught each. Short runs of fuzz tests, which feed a function random input, found nothing.
- **A test that is not steady.** One run of the suite had a single failure in `TestWaitingNodeRefusesAnOldBlock`, a timing test. It then passed 30 times alone and in two whole runs. It is not fixed.
- **Real use.** On the development network the CVN edition of XMRig has mined at a real pool, and the pool's page, the miner's line and the wallet all worked.

## What is known and not done

- **The two seed scripts have not been run on a real server** in their new form. Do one seed first.
- **The full check of a backup has not been timed on a long chain.** The guide's words about minutes and hours are an estimate.
- **The newest pool payment timing has not been run on a real server.** That is the rule that reaches a small miner within a day.
- **Giving through a pool is not built.** It can mean three different things, and two of them change how a pool divides a reward. It waits for a decision.
- **RandomX on arm64 has never been checked, although a `linux_arm64` program is shipped.** Treat that program as untried.
- **No independent human audit** has been done of any of this.
- **The new parts of the wallet window were not looked at on a screen** when they were written, and the screen has no automatic test.
- **A few damaged-file cases are left.** A coin that cannot be read at all is taken for no coin when a transfer is checked. A refused rollback is tried again at every pass. Both are believed to leave a node standing still and not going on with wrong coins. Twice an item on a list like this has turned out worse than believed, so do not take that belief as a promise.
- **A pool's operator can fail to pay,** and a miner's machine cannot read the work it is given. See the pool section.
- **The CVN Testnet has not started.**

## Checking a download

Every file in this release is signed by GitHub as built by this project's build recipe from this repository at this tag: `gh attestation verify cvn_node_windows.zip --repo kalashnikovski/cvn_node`. `SHA256SUMS_windows.txt` and `SHA256SUMS_linux.txt` list the SHA-256 of each package and of each program inside it, and anyone can rebuild the programs from the tag and compare. Section 11 of `Installation_Guide.md` says how.

## Updating

This is not an update that keeps your chain. Start afresh.

1. Stop the miner or seed.
2. Replace the programs with this release's. A seed: see "For anyone running a seed" above, and do one seed first.
3. Delete the old chain file (`cvn_devnet.db` on the development network) and do not put an old backup back. Keep your wallet.
4. Start again as before, with the Bitcoin height and seeds the people who run the network give you.

The fast miner itself is not changed by this release: the version you have still works. It is at https://github.com/kalashnikovski/cvn-xmrig/releases.
